## 3. Naming, messaging, and voice

Use **TaskWavePH** in prose. The supplied logo spells **TASKWAVEPH** in uppercase,
with **WAVE** highlighted in blue in the color wordmark. Keep the artwork's
capitalization and lettering intact.

### Media-kit specification — Taglines

- Primary: **Outsource. Optimize. Grow.**
- Artwork treatment: **OUTSOURCE. OPTIMIZE. GROW.** The stacked example highlights
  **GROW.** in cyan.
- Supporting line: **We handle the tasks. You focus on growth.**
- Partnership line: **Your Partner in Outsourcing. Your Advantage in Growth.**

Keep wording and order intact. Do not replace the primary tagline with a sticker
slogan. Preserve text already embedded in logo files.

### Inferred voice — Website guidance

The materials suggest a clear, confident, professional, and people-focused voice.
Write direct sentences about support, reliable work, efficient processes, and
growth. Use plain language for applicants and factual descriptions for clients.

For recruitment, retain the approved confirmation language in WORKSPACE.md:
contact depends on matching an available opportunity. Cultural references to
remote work or global opportunity do not promise every role is remote or global.

White backgrounds and white reverse lettering also appear in the kit. White is

### Website guidance

- Use navy and readable neutrals to anchor content; use blue and cyan deliberately.
- Keep blue-to-cyan gradients in supplied artwork. The kit does not specify exact
  CSS gradient stops, direction, or animation.
- Check foreground/background contrast for each use, especially blue or cyan text
  on light surfaces. An official brand color is not automatically accessible in
  every text or button combination.
- Keep validation errors, focus indicators, and interaction states clear. Additional
  semantic colors need accessible contrast and must be labeled as UI choices.
- Do not infer new official hex codes from merchandise photographs or mockups.

## 6. Logos, icons, and visual language

### Observed logo treatments

The logo combines a **T** with a flowing **W / wave** motif. Full-color artwork
uses navy, blue, and cyan, including white detailing in the wave. The kit shows
horizontal and stacked arrangements, white reverse treatments, and a standalone
monogram. Use the supplied artwork rather than redrawing its geometry.

The kit's white monogram and monochrome lockups are visible in the reference board
but are not supplied as separate production files in this folder. Do not claim a
standalone white/vector asset exists or recolor a PNG to create an official variant.

### Website guidance — Logo use

- Preserve aspect ratio, colors, embedded tagline, alignment, and source transparency.
- Use the transparent navy/blue horizontal logo on light backgrounds. Use the
  supplied white/blue lockup on its existing black background when appropriate.
- Inspect the standalone monogram against its intended surface: its dark T needs
  enough contrast. Use actual artwork for brand identity; Lucide Waves is a
  foundation placeholder, not the official logo.
- The horizontal PNGs include generous canvas padding. Size for the visible mark,
  not just the file dimensions. Never stretch or crop meaningful artwork.
- Avoid blurry enlargement, distortion, color substitutions, extra effects, or
  rearranging the logo's parts. Obtain a suitable approved source for small or
  specialized placements when the supplied raster artwork is insufficient.
- No official minimum size or measured clear-space rule is supplied. Leave ample
  surrounding space and verify legibility at the actual display size; label any
  numeric size/spacing choices as implementation guidance.

### Website guidance — Imagery and icons

Use blue/cyan wave motifs to suggest momentum and connection. Keep decorative
waves away from form labels, errors, and important controls. Motion, if introduced
later, should respect reduced-motion preferences.

The service/theme icons use simple outlines. Existing Lucide icons may represent
those meanings in UI; they are not extracted official vector artwork. Standalone
service icon files were not present in the supplied logo folder.

Sticker illustrations can inform team culture materials. Merchandise and signage
mockups demonstrate branding applications; they are not documentary photos of
actual offices, teams, facilities, or employee benefits.

## 9. Instructions for future AI work

1. Read WORKSPACE.md for product scope and this document for brand decisions.
2. Use the supplied company story, services, and official tagline faithfully.
3. Use original artwork for brand identity. Do not regenerate, trace, recolor, or
   replace the logo without explicit direction; do not mistake UI icons for logos.
4. Distinguish source specifications, inferred voice, and implementation choices.
   Document uncertainty instead of inventing missing brand rules or company facts.
5. Check appropriate logo backgrounds, aspect ratio, contrast, readability,
   accessibility, and mobile layouts before completing design work.
6. Keep culture slogans and mockup material in context. Confirm social/contact
   details and hiring facts before using them as operational website information.
7. If sources change, inspect the new files, retain provenance, and update this
   reference before propagating new brand decisions through the site.
